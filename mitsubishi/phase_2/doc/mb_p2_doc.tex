\section{Focus Area, Rationale, and Stakeholder Relevance}
This project simulates the core-level Auger electron spectra of extreme ultraviolet (EUV) photoresists to evaluate their chemical sensitivity and performance. During EUV lithography, 92 eV exposure induces core-level ionization (e.g., Iodine 4d shell) which undergoes Auger decay cascades. This process generates low-energy secondary electrons that initiate chemical reactions in the photoresist material. Understanding this cascade is essential for optimizing resist sensitivity and resolution at sub-10 nm nodes.

The primary stakeholders and beneficiaries of this work include:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*,noitemsep,topsep=0pt]
  \item \textbf{Semiconductor Manufacturers and Lithographers:} Guides the selection and tuning of photoresist chemistry to maximize light-absorption and secondary electron yield.
  \item \textbf{Materials Scientists:} Provides a robust computational framework for predicting electron cascade kinetics in halogenated compounds.
  \item \textbf{Advanced Electronics Industry:} Facilitates next-generation microchip patterning at high yield and low exposure doses.
\end{itemize}

\section{Technical Methodology and Hybrid Quantum Integration}
We implement a hybrid quantum-classical chemistry workflow consisting of four key stages: (1) Ground-state reference preparation, (2) Excited-state extraction, (3) Minimal basis projection, and (4) One-Center Approximation (OCA) decay contraction.

\subsection{Ground-State Reference Preparation}
To prepare high-fidelity reference ground states, we employ a Generative Quantum Eigensolver (GQE)~\cite{gqe2024} that generates shallow quantum circuits. We implement two transformer architectures in PyTorch:
\begin{enumerate}[noitemsep,topsep=0pt]
  \item \textbf{GPTQE:} An autoregressive causal model based on nanoGPT that predicts discrete gate sequences.
  \item \textbf{DiTQE:} A Diffusion Transformer-style architecture where the target ground-state energy acts as a continuous condition. DiTQE uses multi-headed self-attention with adaptive RMSNorm to modulate token embeddings, capturing global circuit topology and eliminating directional biases.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Excited-State Extraction via q-sc-EOM}
Using the optimized GQE reference state $|0\rangle = U_{\rm GQE}|{\rm HF}\rangle$ as the vacuum, we extract excited states via the quantum self-consistent Equation-of-Motion (q-sc-EOM) solver~\cite{keithley2026} across two channels:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*,noitemsep,topsep=0pt]
  \item \textbf{Ionization Potential (IP) Space:} Resolves core-ionized doublet states ($N-1$ electrons) for the initial core-hole state $|\Psi_I\rangle$.
  \item \textbf{Double Ionization Potential (DIP) Space:} Resolves doubly-ionized singlet states ($N-2$ electrons) for the final double-hole states $|\Psi_K\rangle$.
\end{itemize}
We compute the transition reduced density matrix (RDM) $\gamma_{pq}(K,I) = \langle \Psi_K | a_c^\dagger a_p a_q | \Psi_I \rangle$, tracking fermionic parity to enforce anti-commutation rules during valence annihilation.

\subsection{Minimal Basis Projection and One-Center Approximation}
The molecular orbital coefficients $C$ are projected onto the minimal basis set (MBS) of the emitter atom (Oxygen/Iodine) via $D = T^{-1} U C$, where $T$ is the MBS overlap matrix and $U$ is the MBS-to-full basis overlap. We contract the transition RDM with projected atomic integrals $V_{pq}$ centered on the emitter atom, yielding transition amplitudes:
\begin{equation}
  A_k = \sum_{p,q} \gamma_{pq}(K, I) V_{pq}
\end{equation}
Applying Fermi's Golden Rule gives the Auger transition rate $\Gamma_k = 2\pi |A_k|^2$. Emitted Auger electron kinetic energy is determined by the difference $E_{\rm kin} = E_I - E_K$. Discrete rates are broadened via a Lorentzian function ($\Gamma = 1.5$~eV) to simulate the spectrum.

\section{Data Modeling and Benchmarking Strategy}
Our data modeling strategy utilizes the standard STO-3G basis set. To scale GQE and q-sc-EOM, we incorporate three acceleration techniques:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*,noitemsep,topsep=0pt]
  \item \textbf{Active Space Selection (AVAS):} Trims the molecular orbitals down to active valence electrons and orbitals (e.g., CAS(24e, 18o) for 36 qubits or CAS(16e, 16o) for 32 qubits in IMePh) to maintain classical tractability.
  \item \textbf{Compressed Double Factorization (CDF):} Compresses the molecular Hamiltonian terms using a Block-Invariant Symmetry Shift (BLISS) and L2-regularized numerical fitting, minimizing the one-norm of the two-body operator.
  \item \textbf{Direct OpenMolcas HDF5 Integration:} Rather than using proxy molecular integrals, we integrate direct multi-center atomic integrals extracted from OpenMolcas HDF5 files~\cite{openmolcas2020} for accurate OCA transition rate modeling.
\end{enumerate}
Validation is conducted on a water (${\rm H_2O}$) benchmark (7 spatial orbitals, 14 qubits) against exact Full Configuration Interaction (FCI) using PySCF~\cite{pyscf2020}.

The DiTQE model converged to a minimum energy of $-75.0276$~Hartrees, achieving a Mean Absolute Error (MAE) of $0.0031$~Hartrees relative to the exact FCI baseline. This confirms that conditioning self-attention blocks on target energy enables the generator to locate shallow, high-fidelity circuit ansatzes. 

Using GQE reference states, q-sc-EOM resolved the excited states. Minimal basis projection and contraction with atomic integrals yielded Auger decay rates. Applying Lorentzian broadening simulated the final spectrum, reproducing the main peak at $E_{\rm kin} = 502.4$~eV, which aligns with experimental Oxygen K-LL Auger emission bands.

Additionally, we simulated the EUV absorption spectrum of the ${\rm H_2O}$ benchmark in the 10--100~eV range using a time-domain Green's function propagated via a second-order Trotter product formula on the factorized Hamiltonian. The quantum-simulated spectrum shows excellent agreement with the exact classical CASCI baseline, resolving the physical valence excitations in the 10--45~eV regime and dropping to a flat zero baseline in the 45--100~eV range. This confirms that ${\rm H_2O}$ has no dipole-allowed transitions near the 92~eV EUV regime in a minimal STO-3G basis, serving as a key validation stepping stone for core-level EUV absorption calculations of candidate photoresists like IMePh (4-iodo-2-methylphenol) where Iodine 4d core-excitations are active at 92~eV.

\section{Quantum Platform Justification and Resource Needs}
Requested: 8xB200-180GB-SXM6 (Alternative: 8xH100-80GB-SXM5).
FP64 state-vector memory: 34 qubits (274.8 GB), 35 qubits (549.7 GB), 36 qubits (1,099.5 GB).
Blackwell (B200-SXM6) Advantages:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*,noitemsep,topsep=0pt]
\item Latency: Halves the GPU count for NVLink distribution vs H100 (2 vs 4 GPUs for 34 qubits); SXM6 NVLink-5 bandwidth (1.8 TB/s) minimizes inter-GPU communication bottleneck.
\item Capacity: 8xB200 offers 1,440 GB VRAM, fitting the 36-qubit space (1,099.5 GB) on a single node. 8xH100 is capped at 640 GB and will go out of memory.
\item Precision: SXM6 native FP64 throughput prevents rounding errors in the kinetic energy distributions.
\end{itemize}

\section{Gap Analysis and Implementation Roadmap}
Our Phase 2 implementation successfully validated the GQE/q-sc-EOM/OCA pipeline on water and established the CUDA-Q and OpenMolcas integration. The Phase 3 roadmap focuses on:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*,noitemsep,topsep=0pt]
  \item \textbf{IMePh Production Scaling:} Apply natural orbital selection and active space truncation to scale GQE execution to the full active space of 4-iodo-2-methylphenol on physical Blackwell GPU clusters.
  \item \textbf{Multi-Center Integrals:} Integrate direct HDF5 tables containing the multi-center integrals from OpenMolcas into the OCA contraction.
\end{itemize}
