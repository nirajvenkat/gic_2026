\section{Focus Area, Rationale, and Stakeholder Relevance}
This project implements a dual extreme ultraviolet (EUV) simulation pipeline to model both the photoabsorption and subsequent core-level Auger electron decay in photoresist materials to evaluate their chemical sensitivity and performance. During EUV lithography, 92 eV exposure first induces photoabsorption to excite or ionize core electrons (e.g., Iodine 4d shell), which subsequently undergo Auger decay cascades. These processes generate low-energy secondary electrons that initiate chemical reactions in the photoresist material. Predicting both the initial absorption and the subsequent electron cascade is essential for optimising resist sensitivity and resolution at sub-10 nm nodes.

The primary stakeholders and beneficiaries of this work include:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*,noitemsep,topsep=0pt]
  \item \textbf{Semiconductor Manufacturers and Lithographers:} Our pipeline guides the selection of photoresist chemistry to maximise light absorption and secondary electron yield, directly impacting exposure dose and throughput.
  \item \textbf{Materials Scientists:} Provides a robust computational framework for predicting electron cascade kinetics in halogenated compounds.
  \item \textbf{Advanced Electronics Industry:} Facilitates next-generation microchip patterning at high yield and low exposure doses.
\end{itemize}

\section{Technical Methodology and Hybrid Quantum Integration}
We implement a dual extreme ultraviolet (EUV) simulation pipeline to model both optical absorption and subsequent electronic decay in photoresist materials. The pipeline couples two core stages in series: (1) an EUV photoabsorption channel utilizing time-domain Green's function propagation on a Compressed Double-Factorized (CDF) Hamiltonian~\cite{kharazi2026}, and (2) an EUV Auger emission channel modeled via a Generative Quantum Eigensolver (GQE) reference state search and quantum self-consistent Equation-of-Motion (q-sc-EOM) solver~\cite{keithley2026}. The two-stage architecture reflects the physical sequence of EUV-induced electron dynamics and ensures that each computational module can be independently validated and scaled.

\subsection{EUV Photoabsorption Spectrum Simulation}
The EUV photoabsorption channel simulates the dipole-allowed valence-excitation spectrum under 92 eV radiation. We compute the transition dipole moment operators $\hat{m}_\rho$ in the active molecular space, and the ground state wavefunction is projected to obtain the initial states $\hat{m}_\rho |I\rangle$ along the Cartesian coordinates. States are propagated in the time-domain under a Compressed Double Factorization (CDF) representation of the Hamiltonian using a second-order Trotter product formula. Measuring the time-dependent expectation values yields the time-domain Green's function $G(t)$, which is Fourier-transformed to resolve the absorption spectrum in the frequency domain.

\subsection{EUV Auger Emission: The GQE Challenge and Decay Modeling}
The Auger emission channel constitutes the primary computational innovation of this work. Auger decay transitions are highly sensitive to the fidelity of the electronic ground-state reference: even small errors in the reference state propagate non-linearly into the computed transition amplitudes and decay rates. Accordingly, preparing a high-fidelity ground-state reference via the GQE represents the central algorithmic challenge, and the quality of this reference directly determines the accuracy of the final Auger spectrum. We address this using a hybrid quantum integration workflow consisting of three stages:

\subsubsection{GQE Ground-State Reference Preparation}
To prepare high-fidelity reference ground states, we employ a Generative Quantum Eigensolver (GQE)~\cite{gqe2024} that generates shallow quantum circuits. Two transformer architectures are implemented in PyTorch and benchmarked against each other:
\begin{enumerate}[noitemsep,topsep=0pt]
  \item \textbf{GPTQE:} An autoregressive causal model based on nanoGPT that predicts discrete gate sequences.
  \item \textbf{DiTQE:} A Diffusion Transformer-style architecture where the target ground-state energy acts as a continuous condition. DiTQE uses multi-headed self-attention with adaptive RMSNorm to modulate token embeddings, capturing global circuit topology and eliminating directional biases.
\end{enumerate}

\subsubsection{Excited-State Extraction via q-sc-EOM}
Using the optimized GQE reference state $|0\rangle = U_{\rm GQE}|{\rm HF}\rangle$ as the vacuum, we extract excited states via the quantum self-consistent Equation-of-Motion (q-sc-EOM) solver~\cite{keithley2026} across two channels:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*,noitemsep,topsep=0pt]
  \item \textbf{Ionization Potential (IP) Space:} Resolves core-ionized doublet states ($N-1$ electrons) for the initial core-hole state $|\text{IP}_I\rangle$.
  \item \textbf{Double Ionization Potential (DIP) Space:} Resolves doubly-ionized singlet states ($N-2$ electrons) for the final double-hole states $|\text{DIP}_K\rangle$.
\end{itemize}
We compute the transition reduced density matrix (RDM) $\gamma_{pq}(K,I) = \langle \text{DIP}_K | a_c^\dagger a_p a_q | \text{IP}_I \rangle$, tracking fermionic parity to enforce anti-commutation rules during valence annihilation.

\subsubsection{Minimal Basis Projection and One-Center Approximation (OCA)}
The molecular orbital coefficients $C$ are projected onto the minimal basis set (MBS) of the emitter atom (Oxygen/Iodine) via $D = T^{-1} U C$, where $T$ is the MBS overlap matrix and $U$ is the MBS-to-full basis overlap. We contract the transition RDM with projected atomic integrals $V_{pq}$ centered on the emitter atom, yielding transition amplitudes:
\begin{equation}
  A_k = \sum_{p,q} \gamma_{pq}(K, I) V_{pq}
\end{equation}
Applying Fermi's Golden Rule gives the Auger transition rate $\Gamma_k = 2\pi |A_k|^2$. Emitted Auger electron kinetic energy is determined by the difference $E_{\rm kin} = E_I - E_K$. Discrete rates are broadened via a Lorentzian function ($\Gamma = 1.5$~eV) to simulate the spectrum.

\section{Data Modeling and Benchmarking Strategy}
Our data modeling strategy utilizes the standard STO-3G basis set. To scale GQE and q-sc-EOM, we incorporate three acceleration techniques:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*,noitemsep,topsep=0pt]
  \item \textbf{Active Space Selection (AVAS):} Trims the molecular orbitals down to active valence electrons and orbitals (e.g., CAS(24e, 18o) for 36 qubits or CAS(16e, 16o) for 32 qubits in IMePh) to maintain classical tractability.
  \item \textbf{Compressed Double Factorization (CDF):} Compresses the molecular Hamiltonian terms using a Block-Invariant Symmetry Shift (BLISS) and L2-regularized numerical fitting, minimizing the one-norm of the two-body operator.
  \item \textbf{Direct OpenMolcas HDF5 Integration:} Rather than using proxy molecular integrals, we integrate direct multi-center atomic integrals extracted from OpenMolcas HDF5 files~\cite{openmolcas2020} for accurate OCA transition rate modeling.
\end{enumerate}
Validation is conducted on a water (${\rm H_2O}$) molecule benchmark (7 spatial orbitals, 14 qubits) against exact Full Configuration Interaction (FCI) using PySCF~\cite{pyscf2020}.


\section{Experimental Results and Discussion}
We first validated the EUV photoabsorption channel. The time-domain Green's function was propagated via a second-order Trotter product formula on the Compressed Double-Factorized (CDF) Hamiltonian. The resolved quantum-simulated absorption spectrum in the 10--100~eV range shows excellent agreement with the exact classical CASCI baseline, resolving the physical valence excitations in the 10--45~eV regime and dropping to a flat zero baseline in the 45--100~eV range. This result confirms that ${\rm H_2O}$ has no dipole-allowed transitions near the 92 eV EUV regime within the minimal STO-3G basis, a physically meaningful and expected result that validates both the CDF-Trotter propagation and the Green's function Fourier pipeline. This photoabsorption validation serves as a key validation stepping stone for core-level EUV absorption calculations of candidate photoresists like IMePh (4-iodo-2-methylphenol) where Iodine 4d core-excitations are active at 92~eV and will constitute the primary spectral feature of interest.

The GPTQE model achieved the best variational performance, reaching a minimum energy of $-74.9650$ Ha with an error of $0.0475$ Ha, while maintaining a tighter energy distribution centered around $-74.9533$ Ha. This indicates that the autoregressive training objective successfully guided the search toward low-energy quantum circuits. In contrast, the DIT model reached $-74.8982$ Ha with an error of $0.1143$ Ha, approximately $2.4\times$ larger than GPTQE. The identical minimum, average, and maximum energies generated by DIT indicate mode collapse, meaning the model repeatedly produced the same circuit regardless of the conditioning signal. This suggests limitations in the conditioning mechanism and highlights the advantage of GPT's causal architecture for learning physically meaningful gate sequences.

Using the GPTQE reference state, the q-sc-EOM framework was applied to compute the core-ionized and doubly-ionized excited states of ${\rm H_2O}$ and simulate its Auger spectrum. Auger transition amplitudes and decay rates were obtained from minimal-basis projections and OpenMolcas atomic integrals. After Lorentzian broadening with $\Gamma = 1.5$~eV, the simulated spectrum reproduced the main emission peak at $502.4$~eV, in close agreement with the experimental Oxygen K-LL Auger band. These results validate the complete GQE$\rightarrow$q-sc-EOM$\rightarrow$OCA workflow and demonstrate that a near-accurate variational reference state can still yield physically meaningful spectroscopic predictions when combined with a self-consistent excited-state solver.

\section{Quantum Platform Justification and Resource Needs}
Requested: 8xB200-180GB-SXM6 (Alternative: 8xH100-80GB-SXM5).
FP64 state-vector memory: 34 qubits (274.8 GB), 35 qubits (549.7 GB), 36 qubits (1,099.5 GB).
Blackwell (B200-SXM6) Advantages:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*,noitemsep,topsep=0pt]
  \item \textbf{Latency}: Halves the GPU count for NVLink distribution vs H100 (2 vs 4 GPUs for 34 qubits); SXM6 NVLink-5 bandwidth (1.8 TB/s) minimizes inter-GPU communication bottleneck.
  \item \textbf{Capacity}: 8xB200 offers 1,440 GB VRAM, fitting the 36-qubit space (1,099.5 GB) on a single node. 8xH100 is capped at 640 GB and will go out of memory.
  \item \textbf{Precision}: SXM6 native FP64 throughput prevents rounding errors in the kinetic energy distributions.
\end{itemize}

\section{Gap Analysis and Implementation Roadmap}
Phase 2 successfully delivered four key milestones: (1) end-to-end validation of the GQE/q-sc-EOM/OCA pipeline on $ {\rm H_2O}$ with physically meaningful Auger spectrum reproduction; (2) benchmarking of two GQE architectures (GPT and DiT) with quantitative energy statistics; (3) CDF-Trotter photoabsorption channel validation against CASCI; and (4) CUDA-Q and OpenMolcas integration establishing the production software stack. The identified gaps and Phase 3 roadmap are as follows:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*,noitemsep,topsep=0pt]
  \item \textbf{IMePh Production Scaling:} Apply natural orbital selection and active space truncation to scale GQE execution to the full active space of 4-iodo-2-methylphenol on physical Blackwell GPU clusters.
  \item \textbf{Pseudopotentials:} Incorporate effective core potentials (ECPs) or pseudopotentials to replace the core electrons of heavy elements like Iodine, which is a critical step to scale both the emission and absorption simulations to large candidate photoresists like IMePh.
\end{itemize}
