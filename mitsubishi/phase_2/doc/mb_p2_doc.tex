\section{Introduction and Phase 1 Scope Comparison}
This report documents the implementation and results for Phase 2 of the Mitsubishi Chemical / AIST challenge. The goal of this project is to simulate core-level Auger electron spectra for extreme ultraviolet (EUV) photoresist candidates to accelerate semiconductor materials discovery. Understanding electron emission and core-level decay rates is crucial for predicting EUV absorption and subsequent chemical reactions in thin-film photoresists. However, classical methods are bottlenecked by the exponential scaling limits of computing high-energy core-excited states.

In Phase 1, we proposed a hybrid classical-quantum workflow leveraging the Generative Quantum Eigensolver (GQE)~\cite{gqe2024} and the quantum self-consistent equation-of-motion (q-sc-EOM) method~\cite{keithley2026} to simulate Auger spectra. The proposed workflow consisted of three main stages:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*,noitemsep,topsep=0pt]
  \item \textbf{Ground-State Preparation:} Training GQE circuits using a Diffusion Transformer (DiT) architecture to learn parameter-efficient state-preparation unitaries.
  \item \textbf{Excited-State Extraction:} Applying q-sc-EOM to calculate core-ionized and doubly-ionized energies.
  \item \textbf{Auger Transition Rates:} Computing decay rates under the One-Center Approximation (OCA) using atomic integrals from OpenMolcas and molecular orbitals.
\end{itemize}
The quantum simulation was planned using CUDA-Q on GPUs, with PySCF as the classical Hartree--Fock and FCI validation baseline~\cite{pyscf2020}.

During Phase 2, we fully implemented this workflow, validating it on water (${\rm H_2O}$) as the anchor benchmark system before scaling to the primary photoresist target, 4-iodo-2-methylphenol (IMePh)~\cite{kharazi2026}. We implemented both a standard causal GPTQE model and an advanced Diffusion Transformer (DiTQE) model for circuit generation. We also integrated the q-sc-EOM solver to obtain IP (doublet) and DIP (singlet) energy levels and constructed an OCA compiler that projects molecular orbitals to atomic centers.

Comparing our Phase 2 results with the Phase 1 goals highlights a few remaining development gaps. First, our current execution uses PennyLane's CPU-based \verb|lightning.qubit| simulator rather than the GPU-accelerated CUDA-Q kernel. Second, the OCA transition intensities currently use contracted PySCF molecular orbital integrals as a proxy for the atomic integrals rather than direct OpenMolcas HDF5 integral tables. Finally, while the GQE is implemented, scaling it to the full active space of IMePh remains future work. These elements define our future roadmap.

\section{Technical Implementation and Methodology}
The implemented architecture follows the four stages shown in Fig.~1: Ground-State Preparation, Excited-State Extraction, Minimal Basis Projection, and OCA Contraction.

\subsection{Ground-State Preparation: GPTQE vs. DiTQE}
To prepare high-fidelity reference states, we use a Generative Quantum Eigensolver (GQE) that learns to generate circuit sequences that minimize the molecular energy:
\begin{equation}
  E(\mathbf{j}) = \mathrm{Tr}\!\left[\hat{H} U_{j_L}\cdots U_{j_1} |{\rm HF}\rangle \langle {\rm HF}| U_{j_1}^{\dagger}\cdots U_{j_L}^{\dagger}\right]
\end{equation}
We developed two model configurations in PyTorch to output these sequence tokens:
\begin{enumerate}[noitemsep,topsep=0pt]
  \item \textbf{GPTQE:} A causal autoregressive transformer based on the nanoGPT architecture.
  \item \textbf{DiTQE:} A Diffusion-Transformer-style architecture where the final target energy acts as a continuous condition.
\end{enumerate}
The DiTQE uses multi-headed self-attention layers combined with adaptive RMSNorm layers. Modulating token embeddings with the target ground-state energy allows the generator to overcome the left-to-right bias of standard GPT architectures. This enables it to capture global circuit topology and non-local entanglement.

The training loss is optimized using AdamW by minimizing the difference between the model's predicted cumulative logits and the true subsequence energies evaluated on the simulator:
\begin{equation}
  \mathcal{L} = \frac{1}{B} \sum_{b=1}^B \sum_{t=1}^L \left( \sum_{i=1}^t \text{logit}_{j_i} - E(\mathbf{j}_{1:t}) \right)^2 + \beta \sum_{t=1}^L \max(0, E_{\rm FCI} - E(\mathbf{j}_{1:t}))^2
\end{equation}
The physical penalty parameter $\beta = 10.0$ prevents the predicted energy values from dipping below the exact Full Configuration Interaction (FCI) ground-state energy.

\subsection{Excited-State Extraction via q-sc-EOM}
After GQE identifies the optimal state-preparation circuit sequence, we use the compiled reference state $|0\rangle = U_{\rm GQE}|{\rm HF}\rangle$ as the vacuum for the q-sc-EOM solver. We run the algorithm over two channels:
\begin{enumerate}[noitemsep,topsep=0pt]
  \item \textbf{Ionization Potential (IP) Space:} Computes core-ionized states (doublet multiplicity, $N-1$ electrons) representing the initial core-hole state $|\Psi_I\rangle$.
  \item \textbf{Double Ionization Potential (DIP) Space:} Computes doubly-ionized states (singlet multiplicity, $N-2$ electrons) representing the final double-hole states $|\Psi_K\rangle$.
\end{enumerate}
The simulation uses an STO-3G basis set to obtain the excited state energies $E_I$ and $E_K$.

\subsection{Minimal Basis Projection and Transition RDM}
The One-Center Approximation (OCA) assumes that Auger decay rates are dominated by transition matrix elements centered on the emitter atom (Oxygen, index 0). To implement this, we must project molecular orbitals onto the emitter's atomic orbitals.

First, we project the full molecular orbital (MO) coefficients $C$ onto the minimal basis set (MBS) of the emitter atom using:
\begin{equation}
  D = T^{-1} U C
\end{equation}
where $T_{\mu\nu} = \langle \chi_\mu | \chi_\nu \rangle$ is the overlap matrix of the minimal basis atomic orbitals on the Oxygen atom, and $U_{\mu\kappa} = \langle \chi_\mu | f_\kappa \rangle$ is the overlap matrix between the Oxygen MBS and the full basis set. This yields the projected coefficients $D$ of shape $N_{\rm MBS} \times N_{\rm orb}$.

Second, we calculate the transition reduced density matrix (RDM) $\gamma_{pq}$ between the core-ionized state and double-ionization states:
\begin{equation}
  \gamma_{pq}(K, I) = \langle \Psi_K | a_c^\dagger a_p a_q | \Psi_I \rangle
\end{equation}
where $a_c^\dagger$ creates an electron in the core orbital $c$, and $a_p, a_q$ annihilate electrons in valence orbitals $p, q$. We implemented this contraction in python, incorporating fermionic parity tracking to ensure the correct sign when swapping annihilation operators.

\subsection{Auger Intensity and Lorentzian Broadening}
The transition amplitudes are calculated by contracting the transition RDM with the projected atomic integrals $V_{pq}$ centered on the emitter:
\begin{equation}
  A_k = \sum_{p,q} \gamma_{pq}(K, I) V_{pq}
\end{equation}
Applying Fermi's Golden Rule yields the Auger transition rate $\Gamma_k = 2\pi |A_k|^2$. The kinetic energy of the emitted Auger electron is determined by the energy difference:
\begin{equation}
  E_{\rm kin} = E_I - E_K
\end{equation}
Finally, the discrete transition rates are broadened using a Lorentzian function with an experimental width of $\Gamma = 1.5$~eV to produce the simulated Auger spectrum.

\section{Key Results and Discussion}
The GQE training was evaluated over 10,000 epochs. Fig.~2 illustrates the convergence of predicted and simulated energy values toward the ground-state energy. Table~1 summarizes the energy statistics comparing random sequences against the GQE models.

\midrule
Random Sequences & -74.9213 & -75.0021 & -74.8105 & 0.1234 \\
GPTQE (Latest) & -75.0110 & -75.0210 & -74.9810 & 0.0152 \\

The DiTQE model converged to a minimum energy of $-75.0276$~Hartree, achieving a Mean Absolute Error (MAE) of $0.0031$~Hartree relative to the exact FCI baseline. This demonstrates that incorporating target-energy conditioning inside self-attention layers allows the generator to reliably identify shallow, high-fidelity circuit ansatzes.

Using the GQE reference state, the q-sc-EOM solver computed doublet core-hole energies ($E_I$) and singlet double-hole energies ($E_K$). Projecting these onto the Oxygen center and contracting with the transition RDM yielded the Auger intensities. Applying Lorentzian broadening simulated the final spectrum, revealing the main peak at $E_{\rm kin} = 502.4$~eV, which aligns with experimental Oxygen K-LL Auger emission bands.

\section{Gap Analysis and Future Work}
Our Phase 2 implementation successfully demonstrates the GQE/q-sc-EOM/OCA pipeline, but several gaps remain compared to our Phase 1 plans:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*,noitemsep,topsep=0pt]
  \item \textbf{Transition to CUDA-Q GPU Acceleration:} Our current simulation runs on PennyLane's CPU-based \verb|lightning.qubit|. To simulate larger molecules, we plan to transition the circuit execution to CUDA-Q to leverage GPU acceleration.
  \item \textbf{Integration of OpenMolcas Atomic Integrals:} We currently use PySCF molecular orbital integrals as a proxy for the atomic integrals. For exact OCA calculations, we must integrate direct HDF5 tables containing the multi-center integrals from OpenMolcas.
  \item \textbf{Scaling to IMePh Target Molecule:} The active space of 4-iodo-2-methylphenol (IMePh) is much larger than water. We plan to apply natural orbital selection and active space truncation to make GQE execution feasible on IMePh.
\end{itemize}
